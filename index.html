Fossil Layers and Mass Extinction Explorer

Explore fictional ecosystems, inspect rock layers and fossil diagrams, infer possible extinction boundaries, and test how environmental change can affect survival.

1. Choose an ecosystem

Student flow
  1. Choose an ecosystem and explore its strata.
  2. Inspect at least five layers, comparing fossil diagrams and annotations.
  3. Tap or click where you think the extinction boundary lies. Evidence may be clear in some ecosystems and uncertain in others.
  4. Try again if needed. The answer is revealed after five attempts, with a brief explanation.
  5. Set up a simulation by choosing an environmental factor and adjusting rate and severity.
  6. Watch an automatic visualisation with a changing landscape and labelled indicators.
  7. Compare runs side by side: survival, biodiversity over time, and resulting fossil layers.

All ecosystems, species, and results are fictional and simplified. Fossils are clues; preservation varies and the record is incomplete. Results are temporary and clear when you reset or close the page.

2–3. Inspect strata and mark a boundary

Inspect each layer's annotated diagram and rock sample. Older layers are lower; younger layers are higher. Use the annotations to infer changes in fossil abundance and diversity rather than relying on exact counts.

Tap or click the extinction boundary

Choose the place where the evidence suggests a major change in fossil life. In some ecosystems the boundary is clear; in others the change may span several layers, so a range of nearby choices can be reasonable.

6. Compare runs side by side

Each time you run the simulation, the result is saved here for comparison until you reset or close the page. Compare environmental settings, survival, biodiversity over time, and resulting fossil layers.

Evidence note: A drop in fossil abundance or diversity can suggest extinction, but preservation varies and the fossil record is incomplete. Ask: what evidence in the diagrams led you to choose a boundary?
